Someday We’ll Die

Privacy Policy

Last updated 9 September 2026

Someday We’ll Die is an app for keeping track of things you want to do and things you have done. This policy explains what it stores, who it is sent to, and how to get rid of it. It is written to be read rather than survived.

The short version. Your lists, photos and messages are stored so the app can show them back to you and to people you deliberately share a list with. They are not sold, not used for advertising, and not used to train anybody’s models. You can export everything and delete the whole account from inside the app.

2. What is stored

Because you typed it

  • Account details — your email address, display name and username. The email address is how you sign in and how a password reset reaches you.
  • Your date of birth. Asked once, when the account is created, because the app is not for under-16s and we would rather ask than assume. We keep the date so that we can show the question was asked and answered. It is not used for anything else — not for advertising, not for personalisation, and it is never shown to other users.
  • Your lists and activities — names, notes, target dates, priorities, links, and how an activity went once you finished it.
  • Photos and video you attach to a completed activity, and a profile photo if you set one.
  • Places — a location on an activity, and one saved home address if you set one. Both are stored as text and as coordinates.
  • Messages and notes you write in a shared list.
  • The “About you” paragraph, if you write one. It is used for one thing only, described in section 5.

Because the app needs it to work

  • A session token, so you stay signed in.
  • When you accepted the terms, and which version of them was on screen at the time.
  • A push subscription if you turn on reminders — an anonymous endpoint issued by your browser or device, plus which browser it was, so an old one can be recognised and removed.
  • Read state for conversations, so unread counts work.
  • A copy of your data on your own device, so the app works offline. It is deleted when you sign out.

If you report somebody

  • The report — who reported, who was reported, why, when, and a copy of the content complained of. See section 8 for how long each part is kept.
  • Who you have blocked. This is visible only to you; the person blocked is not told and cannot find out.

What is deliberately not collected

  • No analytics, no tracking pixels, no advertising identifiers, and no third-party trackers of any kind.
  • No cookies used for tracking. The app stores a sign-in token and a few preferences on your own device. Nothing is shared with anyone and there is nothing to consent to, which is why there is no cookie banner.
  • No contacts, no calendar, no health data, no browsing history.
  • No background location. The app asks for your location only if you tap Current location while choosing a place, and it is used for that one search and not stored.
  • Photo metadata is read on your device to offer a location for a photo you just attached. If you decline the offer, nothing is stored.
  • No fonts, scripts or styles loaded from anybody else’s servers. Everything the app needs to draw itself is served from the app’s own address, so opening it does not hand your IP address to a third party you never chose.

5. About the AI features

When you create an activity, the app sends its name to Anthropic’s Claude API to guess two things: whether the name identifies a specific place, and roughly how hard the activity would be for you. To judge the second it also sends your home city, your “About you” paragraph if you wrote one, and a small sample of activities you already have with the ratings they carry.

  • It happens when you create an activity, not when you edit one.
  • Your photos, messages, notes, date of birth and email address are never sent.
  • Anthropic does not use data submitted through its API to train its models.
  • If you leave “About you” empty, nothing about you personally is sent beyond a home city.
  • No decision with a legal or similarly significant effect is made about you automatically. The guesses only fill in two fields, and you can change both.

6. Shared lists

Sharing a list is a deliberate act: you create an invite and send it to someone. Once they accept, everyone in that list can see the list, its activities, the photos and notes attached to them, and the conversation, along with your display name and profile photo. They cannot see your email address, your date of birth, your home address, or anything in your other lists.

A message you send stays in the conversation for the other members even if you later delete your account, because removing one side of a discussion makes it unreadable for the people who had it. It is shown as coming from a deleted account.

7. Children

The app is not for anyone under 16. We ask for a date of birth when an account is created and do not knowingly create or keep an account for someone younger. If you believe a child has an account here, write to [YOUR CONTACT EMAIL] and we will remove it and everything in it.

8. How long it is kept

  • While your account exists — your lists, activities, media and messages are kept until you delete them or delete the account.
  • Deleted media — removing a photo from an activity removes it from the activity immediately; the stored file is cleared separately during periodic maintenance.
  • Invites expire after 30 days.
  • Reports — the copy of the reported content is deleted 180 days after the report is resolved. The record that a report was made is kept so that repeated behaviour can be recognised.
  • Backups age out on their own within 30 days.

9. Your choices

  • Get a copy. Settings → Export My Data downloads everything on your account as a JSON file, straight from the app. If you would rather we sent it, write to [YOUR CONTACT EMAIL].
  • Delete everything. Settings → Delete Account. It removes your account, your lists, your activities, your media and your reminders. A list you own and shared is deleted for everyone in it; a list you joined is simply left, and the other members keep it. This cannot be undone.
  • Correct something. Almost everything is editable in the app; anything that is not, we will fix on request.
  • Object, or restrict what we do. Write to us and say what you object to and why.
  • Withdraw consent for the AI features by clearing the “About you” paragraph, and for notifications in your device settings.

If you are in the UK, EU, or a US state with a privacy statute, these rights are yours by law and this is how to use them. We answer within 30 days, we do not charge for it, and we will not treat you differently for asking. You may also complain to your local data protection authority — in the UK that is the Information Commissioner’s Office.

10. Security

Data is encrypted in transit. Database access is enforced per-account at the database level, so one account cannot read another’s rows. Passwords are stored hashed by our authentication provider and are never visible to us. No system is perfect; if something goes wrong that affects you, we will tell you and the relevant regulator, within 72 hours of becoming aware where the law requires it.

11. Where data is held

Data is stored on servers operated by the providers in section 4, which may be located outside your country, including in the United States. Transfers out of the UK and EEA rely on the providers’ standard contractual clauses, and on the UK Addendum where it applies.

12. Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, the date at the top changes and the app tells you the next time you open it.
