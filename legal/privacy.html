Someday We’ll Die

Privacy Policy

Last updated 31 August 2026

Someday We’ll Die is an app for keeping track of things you want to do and things you have done. This policy explains what it stores, who it is sent to, and how to get rid of it. It is written to be read rather than survived.

The short version. Your lists, photos and messages are stored so the app can show them back to you and to people you deliberately share a list with. They are not sold, not used for advertising, and not used to train anybody’s models. You can delete the whole account, and everything in it, from inside the app.

1. Who is responsible

The app is operated by Landon James, 7474 SW 49th Ct. Questions, requests and complaints go to landon.talus@gmail.com.

2. What is stored

Because you typed it

  • Account details — your email address, display name and username. The email address is how you sign in and how a password reset reaches you.
  • Your lists and activities — names, notes, target dates, priorities, links, and how an activity went once you finished it.
  • Photos and video you attach to a completed activity, and a profile photo if you set one.
  • Places — a location on an activity, and one saved home address if you set one. Both are stored as text and as coordinates.
  • Messages and notes you write in a shared list.
  • The “About you” paragraph, if you write one. It is used for one thing only, described in section 4.

Because the app needs it to work

  • A session token, so you stay signed in.
  • A push subscription if you turn on reminders — an anonymous endpoint issued by your browser or device, plus which browser it was, so an old one can be recognised and removed.
  • Read state for conversations, so unread counts work.
  • A copy of your data on your own device, so the app works offline. It is deleted when you sign out.

What is deliberately not collected

  • No analytics, no tracking pixels, no advertising identifiers, and no third-party trackers of any kind.
  • No contacts, no calendar, no health data, no browsing history.
  • No background location. The app asks for your location only if you tap Current location while choosing a place, and it is used for that one search and not stored.
  • Photo metadata is read on your device to offer a location for a photo you just attached. If you decline the offer, nothing is stored.

3. Who it is sent to

The app runs on a small number of services. Each one receives only what it needs to do its job.

Nothing is sold. Nothing is shared with advertisers or data brokers. Data is disclosed to anyone else only if the law requires it, and only to the extent it requires.

4. About the AI features

When you create an activity, the app sends its name to Anthropic’s Claude API to guess two things: whether the name identifies a specific place, and roughly how hard the activity would be for you. To judge the second it also sends your home city, your “About you” paragraph if you wrote one, and a small sample of activities you already have with the ratings they carry.

  • It happens when you create an activity, not when you edit one.
  • Your photos, messages, notes and email address are never sent.
  • Anthropic does not use data submitted through its API to train its models.
  • If you leave “About you” empty, nothing about you personally is sent beyond a home city.

5. Shared lists

Sharing a list is a deliberate act: you create an invite and send it to someone. Once they accept, everyone in that list can see the list, its activities, the photos and notes attached to them, and the conversation, along with your display name and profile photo. They cannot see your email address, your home address, or anything in your other lists.

A message you send stays in the conversation for the other members even if you later delete your account, because removing one side of a discussion makes it unreadable for the people who had it. It is shown as coming from a deleted account.

6. How long it is kept

  • While your account exists — your lists, activities, media and messages are kept until you delete them or delete the account.
  • Deleted media — removing a photo from an activity removes it from the activity immediately; the stored file is cleared separately during periodic maintenance.
  • Invites expire after 30 days.
  • Reports of content are kept after review so that repeated behaviour can be recognised.

7. Your choices

  • Delete everything. You → Delete Account. It removes your account, your lists, your activities, your media and your reminders. A list you own and shared is deleted for everyone in it; a list you joined is simply left, and the other members keep it. This cannot be undone.
  • Get a copy. Email landon.talus@gmail.com and we will send you your data in a machine-readable format.
  • Correct something. Almost everything is editable in the app; anything that is not, we will fix on request.
  • Turn off notifications in your device settings at any time, or mute one conversation from inside it.

If you are in the UK, EU, or a US state with a privacy statute, these rights are yours by law and this is how to use them. We answer within 30 days. You may also complain to your local data protection authority.

8. Security

Data is encrypted in transit. Database access is enforced per-account at the database level, so one account cannot read another’s rows. Passwords are stored hashed by our authentication provider and are never visible to us. No system is perfect; if something goes wrong that affects you, we will tell you.

9. Children

The app is not intended for children under 13, and accounts are not knowingly created for them. If you believe a child has created one, contact us and it will be removed.

10. Where data is held

Data is stored on servers operated by the providers in section 3, which may be located outside your country. Transfers out of the UK and EEA rely on the providers’ standard contractual clauses.

11. Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, the date at the top changes and the app tells you the next time you open it.
